Corporations Are Software Junkyards

2026-11-06 07:06:20 GMT

Let's start this by talking about the biggest corporate engineering meme prior to AI , i.e. microservices. Need I say it?... This was a monumentally bad idea.

My opinion is this design evolved out of:

  1. The lack of training & upskilling in the modern workplace
  2. The subsequent need to isolate mistakes
  3. Astronaut architecture

My first introduction to microservices in "professional" software was at Grab. Grab is the dominant ride hailing app, it's the Uber of South East Asia. I'm sure that there are excellent engineering teams at Grab, but unfortunately due to my lack of experience in web dev and my lack of practice in algorithms I did not make cut for these teams and was shunted to what I can only describe as a managerial cesspit. I'm not a CS grad that had the luxury of their student years to grind leetcode for years. I had trained on the job and algorithms are a stupid test for programmer competency anyway.

The reason why I say this is because my direct manager and the skip level manager where not proficient technically. The skip level manager was an MBA grad and the direct manager had only 3 years of professional coding experience and was 6 years into his career.

As such they were unable to provide technical guidance and most of their work was occupied with back and forth negotiations in meetings trying to communicate ideas and create structures around an implementation they do not understand. As such most of their work results in misinformation.

This need not be a negative if the managers got their hands dirty with the code base or allowed the engineer to iterate on their own designs at a smaller scale. However due to the need for visibility and headlines to progress their main goal, career advancement, they also architect...

This combination of hierarchically superiority, technical inferiority and flashy KPIs is a ripe environment for the creation of astronaut architecture. What is that you might ask? In simple terms, it's people creating diagrams instead of writing code.

So it was announced after our "senior leadership" spent some time huddled in gatekept meetings while the real engineers fought with previous iterations of slop architecture, that we had our new North Star!

A new diagram to solve the previous architecture! It was at this moment I realized that these poeple had no idea what they were doing. I had spent the next 2 years of career there constantly fighting against this "bad engineering" while I was constantly framed as being combative. This is because as I was told again and again working about making good impressions, not one manager I had talked to cared about the underlying quality of work.

A memorable moment that always brings a dark smile to my lips was when my skip level manager, audibly lost his shit when I had presented an analysis of the design of certain microservice and data dependency hell that resulted. His exact words were "what's wrong with! it's input output". This was a man in his mid 40s, who had previous worked as a manager at Cisco, was a CTO in India and had an MBA. MBA engineering at its finest... Grim...

I was even shamed publicly by a higher level manager in front of other colleagues because of the political machinations engineered by the career manager, I reported to. This direct manager even pressured me to say nothing to our COO when she came to visit our office. Years later after I had rescued 3 separate projects, this same manager refused to assign any new work to me, effectively freezing my career. When I mentioned this in our 1 on 1s, he said to me that "I was unable to do anything". Suffice to say he was sociopathic corporate rat, who used his shallow engineering knowledge to impress his non-technical counterparts. In his presentation on himself, his main engineering contribution was a helper function in the house Go testing library. All this function did was swap function pointers in a test's execution context.

If you're still here you may be wondering, what does all of this have to do with engineering?! Nothing, and that my friends is why corporations are software junkyards. It's where engineering dies.
